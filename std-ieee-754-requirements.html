IEEE 754-2019 requirements

The standard is paywalled and was not obtained. No clause of IEEE Std 754-2019 is quoted here from the standard itself, and no clause number is given unless a secondary source states it. Every item below is marked with the secondary source it is taken from:

  • [SA] the IEEE SA abstract page of 754-2019 (specs/number-formats/ieee754-abstract.html).
  • [WG] the IEEE 754 working group's public note Conformance and options (specs/number-formats/ieee754-conformance-and-options.txt, from https://grouper.ieee.org/groups/msc/ANSI_IEEE-Std-754-2019/background/).
  • [LLVM] LLVM APFloat at llvmorg-23.1.0: semIEEEhalf = {15, -14, 11, 16} and semIEEEsingle = {127, -126, 24, 32} (maximum exponent, minimum exponent, precision with the hidden bit, size in bits; APFloat.cpp:71, :73; the field meanings at APFloat.h:1005-1016 say they "match the definition of IEEE 754"); fltNonfiniteBehavior::IEEE754 (APFloat.h:959-963); IEEEFloat::handleOverflow (APFloat.cpp:1490).
  • [MX] OCP MX v1.1 §4.1, which defines Float32 as "Binary32 format defined in the IEEE Standard for Floating-Point Arithmetic" and roundTiesToEven as "a method for rounding to nearest, where ties are broken by rounding them to the value with an even least-significant digit. See IEEE Standard for Floating-Point Arithmetic [4], Section 4.3.1".
  • [OFP8] OCP OFP8 Revision 1.1 §4 and §4.1, which name binary16 and binary32 as IEEE formats and take the terms subnormal, NaN and trailing significand field from IEEE 754.
  • [AMD] the FP16 row of Table 30, AMD CDNA3 ISA guide §7.2: "E5M10, bias 15, +0 0x0000, -0 0x8000, INF 0x7C00, -INF 0xFC00, max 65504, min (norm) 6.10352E-05, min (denorm) 5.96046E-08".

The abstract [SA] states the standard "specifies interchange and arithmetic formats and methods for binary and decimal floating-point arithmetic" and "exception conditions and their default handling". Our scope is the binary16 and binary32 interchange encodings, their NaN and Inf, and conversion with roundTiesToEven.

item requirement source
IEEE-R1 binary16 encoding. 1 sign bit, 5 exponent bits with bias 15, 10 trailing significand bits; emax 15, emin −14, precision 11; largest finite 65504, smallest normal 2^−14, smallest subnormal 2^−24. [LLVM] semIEEEhalf; [AMD] Table 30
IEEE-R2 binary32 encoding. 1 sign bit, 8 exponent bits with bias 127, 23 trailing significand bits; emax 127, emin −126, precision 24. [LLVM] semIEEEsingle; [MX] §4.1
IEEE-R3 Subnormals. An encoding with a zero exponent field and a non-zero trailing significand is subnormal: magnitude 2^emin × (0 + M × 2^(1−p)). [OFP8] §4.1 (term from IEEE 754); [MX] §4.1 definition of subnormal; [AMD] min (denorm)
IEEE-R4 Signed zeros. +0 and −0 are distinct encodings (binary16 0x0000 and 0x8000). [AMD] Table 30
IEEE-R5 Infinities. An all-ones exponent field with a zero trailing significand is ±Inf (binary16 0x7C00 and 0xFC00). [LLVM] fltNonfiniteBehavior::IEEE754: "A value is nonfinite if the exponent field is all 1s. In such cases, a value is Inf if the significand bits are all zero, and NaN otherwise"; [AMD] Table 30
IEEE-R6 NaNs. An all-ones exponent field with a non-zero trailing significand is NaN, of either sign. A conversion of a NaN delivers a NaN; a converted signalling NaN delivers a quiet NaN. [LLVM] as IEEE-R5, and IEEEFloat::convert (APFloat.cpp:2450), which returns opInvalidOp for a signalling source and makes the result quiet
IEEE-R7 roundTiesToEven conversion. A conversion to a narrower format rounds to the nearest representable value; a tie goes to the value with an even least significant digit. [MX] §4.1, citing IEEE 754-2019 §4.3.1
IEEE-R8 Overflow under roundTiesToEven delivers ±Inf, with the sign of the source. [LLVM] handleOverflow: "Sign is preserved. We either become infinity or the largest finite number", infinity for rmNearestTiesToEven
IEEE-R9 Widening is exact. Every binary16 value is a binary32 value, so binary16 to binary32 is exact. [LLVM] the two semantics: binary32's precision and exponent range contain binary16's

Outside the scope

Exception flags and their default handling, the other rounding-direction attributes, arithmetic operations, NaN payload propagation, binary64 and wider, and the decimal formats. The working group note [WG] says new 2019 features are "always recommended rather than required" (for example minimum/maximum operations); none is in scope.
